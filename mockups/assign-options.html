Three Ways to Assign

Same school, same goals, same six people in all three. What changes is where a leader enters, and that changes what they end up doing.

What this encourages

A leader cannot reach a tool without passing through a goal, so the why is structural rather than a field on a form. The goals that have never been measured are visible as empty, which is a standing invitation to fix them.

What it risks

A principal who already knows the tool they want has to go and find the right goal first, which can feel like paperwork. And nothing shows one person's total load until you are deep in the picker, so it is the easiest of the three in which to overload somebody.

What this encourages

It is the only one of the three that models collaborative time rather than individual homework. One tool, one group, one date everybody shares, so the thing being discussed in a staff meeting is the same thing. Volume stays low without a cap doing the work.

What it risks

Hardest to differentiate. A leader who wants one teacher on one thing has to make a group of one, which feels odd. And the per person ceiling of three becomes invisible, so you can hit it by accident from the group side.
