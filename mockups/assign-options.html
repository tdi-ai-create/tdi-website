Assign a tool

Three ways in. Open any one to see what it is good at, what it risks, and to assign from there. They all end in the same place, so pick whichever matches what you are already thinking about.

Starting from a goal

What this encourages

You cannot reach a tool without passing through a goal, so the reason is built in rather than added afterwards. The goals with no reading sit in plain sight, which is a standing nudge to go and fix one.

What it risks

You cannot see how much any one person is already carrying until you reach the list of names, so this is the easiest of the three in which to overload somebody. The dots on each name are there to catch it.

Starting from shared time

What this encourages

The only one that plans around a meeting rather than around individuals. Everyone works on the same thing, so the conversation in the room is about one tool and the date is a real date you already hold.

What it risks

Hardest to tailor. If one teacher needs something different, do that one separately rather than widening the group until the tool fits nobody particularly well.
