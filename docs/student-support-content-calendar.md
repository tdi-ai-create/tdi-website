# Student support content: build and publish calendar

Teacher-facing tools for the moments a student's emotional state lands in a
classroom. Approved 2026-09-01.

**Everything here is teacher-facing. Nothing in this programme is written for
students**, and nothing is a lesson. The teacher is the reader, always.

## Why this exists

Across 268 published Quick Wins, measured 2026-09-01:

| Topic | TDI tools |
|---|---|
| Mental health | **0** |
| Trauma | **0** |
| Grief and loss | **0** |
| Bullying | **0** |
| Conflict | 1 |
| Emotional regulation | 3 |

268 tools and almost nothing on the emotional and behavioural side of a
classroom. That is not a gap in a category, it is a missing category.

Meanwhile a partner's student-facing SEL library of 60 modules covers exactly
this ground, evenly across PreK-2, 3-5 and 6-12:

| Their topic | Modules touching it |
|---|---|
| Mental health | 52 |
| Trauma affected students | 45 |
| Emotional regulation | 40 |
| Conflict resolution | 40 |
| Peer support | 38 |
| Anti-bullying | 32 |
| Family changes | 29 |
| Grief and loss | 17 |

Their content teaches the student. When a module on grief runs, **the teacher is
left in the room with whatever it surfaced** and has nothing. That is the gap
this programme fills, and it is TDI's whole positioning.

## The unit is a teacher moment, not a topic

Sixty modules do not become sixty tools. The teacher's need repeats even when the
student topic changes: "a student told me something and I was not ready" is one
tool serving dozens of their modules.

**Target: 12 to 18 tools for the whole space.** That is what makes this
achievable rather than theoretical.

## Cadence

**Two per month.** Deliberately modest.

The QA bottleneck is one reviewer, and this content needs more review than a
checklist, not less. Two a month puts the full set inside a school year. Going
faster than review can verify is what produced 181 unreviewed published items in
the first place.

## Sequence, and why this order

**Start where the expert is not needed, so the expert does not block the start.**

### Phase 1, months 1 to 3: in the moment

No clinical exposure. Practical, high frequency, agent-reviewable under the
sourcing skill alone. This phase proves the format and builds the pipeline.

1. Emotional regulation, what to do in the ninety seconds
2. Conflict between two students, in class, right now
3. Re-entry after a student has been out of the room
4. Regulation for the whole room, not one child
5. Conflict that keeps recurring between the same pair
6. What to say when a student shuts down

### Phase 2, months 4 to 6: policy-adjacent

Safe to write, but each one touches district policy and mandated reporting, so
each names the policy rather than restating law.

7. Bullying, what you are seeing and what to do about it
8. The reporting conversation, with a student and with an adult
9. Peer exclusion that is not bullying but is not nothing
10. Family change, what a teacher can and cannot ask
11. A student who has just moved schools

### Phase 3, months 7 to 9: needs a credentialed human

**These do not publish on agent review alone.** Highest teacher need, highest
risk, and the only place a person's credentials are genuinely required.

12. A student discloses self-harm
13. A student discloses suicidal ideation
14. A student discloses abuse
15. Grief after a death, the class and the child
16. Signs you are seeing and what to do with them, notice and refer
17. Supporting a trauma-affected student across a term
18. Your own capacity, when the teacher is the one struggling

## What has to be true before Phase 3 starts

A named credentialed reviewer for those four disclosure and grief topics. Not a
standing retainer, a sign-off on seven items and the source list. If that person
does not exist by month 6, Phase 3 waits. It does not ship on agent review.

## How each tool gets built

Governed by `docs/hub-content-standard.md` plus the sourced-support skill now in
Jasmine's, Julie Lynn's and Nora's instructions.

1. **Draft.** Jasmine, working from approved sources only. Every instruction
   traceable to NCTSN, SAMHSA, CDC, AAP, ASCA, NASP, peer-reviewed research or a
   district's own policy. An unsourceable instruction stops the draft rather than
   being written around.
2. **Review.** Julie Lynn rejects any unsourced instruction before looking at
   anything else, then applies the usual bar: read-through cold, tool_type
   honest, usability, specificity, school appropriate.
3. **Phase 3 only.** Credentialed human sign-off before publish.
4. **Publish** through `review_published` with `file_bytes`, `tool_file_bytes`
   and evidence, like everything else.

## Format

Reference cards carry most of this. In the moment, what to say, what not to say,
who to tell. Checklists where there is a sequence to follow.

**One course**, not many. Trauma-informed basics is the only part that genuinely
needs sequencing, and it comes after Phase 3 has produced the material.

**No quizzes and no games on these topics.** A quiz about grief is tonally
wrong, and the format would undercut the content.

## What tells us it worked

Not the number shipped. That measure is what produced a library nobody had read.

- Are these tools opened, and by whom
- Do teachers come back to the same one twice, which is the signal a reference
  card is doing its job
- Do partner districts ask for them by name
- Reports filed against them, now that reporting exists for Quick Wins

Set the baseline before Phase 1 publishes, so the comparison is real.
