School Visit Prep SOP

What we ask a school for before an observation day, how we ask, and what we owe them afterward.

Created 29 September 2026 · Use for every observation day so no school gets a thinner version than another

The shape of it

1

As soon as the date is confirmed

Send the prep email

Template at the bottom of this page. It does three jobs at once: asks for what we need, takes the fear out of the word observation, and shows them what they are getting.

Send it early. A roster request two days out is a favour you are asking. Three weeks out it is just planning.

2

Once their answers come back

Send the room by room route

Times against rooms, building by building. This is what makes the day feel organised rather than like someone wandering the halls. It also protects the people we are there for: a named route means nobody gets skipped because their room was awkward to reach.

3

On the day

Notepad only

Eyes up, fully present, nothing between us and the room. Where the schedule allows, sit with people one to one after observing them.

4

Same day, after the visit

Photograph the notepad and upload

Pages go into the Leadership Dashboard on the partnership page, under Observations and Love Notes in the Reports sidebar. Handwriting is read and matched against the roster, and Love Notes are drafted per educator.

This is why the roster matters more than anything else on the ask list. No roster means no name to match to, and a Love Note addressed to nobody is not a Love Note.

5

Within 24 hours

Love Notes sent, dashboard populated

Drafts reviewed and edited before anything goes out. Leadership gets the summary, the themes across buildings, and the building by building view.

24 hours is the promise made in the prep email, so it is a commitment and not an aspiration. If it cannot be met for a given visit, take the line out of the template for that school rather than letting it slide.

What we ask for, and why

AskPriorityWhy we need it
Roster: names, roles, building Matters most It is what lets us write to people by name afterward instead of writing to a room. Without it the whole post-visit half of the day collapses.
Address for every building Helps a lot Multi-building days fall apart on travel time. Include the district office if we are starting there.
School day start and end times Helps a lot So we are not arriving before the doors open or losing the last block. Varies between buildings more than people expect.
The day's schedule Nice to have Lets us build the route around where people actually are. If it is hard to pull, we build the route on arrival instead.

Say out loud that none of it is a blocker

A principal reading a list of four requests from a vendor hears homework. Tell them to send whatever is easy and that we will work around the rest, and mean it. We have run good days on a roster and nothing else.

Handling the word observation

Schools hear "we are coming to observe your staff" and picture evaluation, because that is what the word means everywhere else in their working life. If we do not address it, the staff room does it for us before we arrive.

Name it directly in the prep email, without being asked. Three things do the work:

  • It is growth focused, not evaluative, and it sits completely separate from their appraisal process.
  • Nothing we write ends up in anyone's file.
  • We are there to catch people doing something well, which is a different job from rating them.

House rules that apply here

  • No internal numbers going outside. Implementation rates, user counts and partner figures stay in. Percentages or words like most are fine, and anything already published on our own site is fine.
  • No other district named. Never illustrate a point with another client. The example dashboard link exists precisely so you do not have to.
  • One email per person. If the school already has something pending from us, fold this into it rather than sending a second.
  • Keep money separate. An overdue invoice does not belong in a visit prep email. Chase it on its own, by phone.
  • Nothing sends without review. Drafts go to Rae before they reach a school.

Subject: Your DATE visit, and a few things that would help

FIRST NAME,

Really looking forward to DAY AND DATE. ONE WARM, SPECIFIC LINE ABOUT WHO THIS DAY IS FOR AND WHY IT MATTERS. For a para focused day: paras are the group most likely to go a whole year without anyone stopping to notice what they do well, so a day built around them is my favorite kind to run.

Here is how it works, what would help me from your end, and where you can go see the whole thing for yourself before I show up.

A few things that would help

None of this is a blocker, so please do not let it turn into a job. Send me whatever is easy and I will work around the rest.

The one that matters most is a ROLE roster: names, roles, and which building each person is in. That is what lets me write to people by name afterward instead of writing to a room.

Beyond that, the address for every building we will be in, your school day start and end times so I am not arriving before the doors open or losing the last block, and the day's schedule if it is simple to pull.

Once I have that, you get back a room by room route with times on it. Nobody spends the day wondering where I am, and nobody gets missed.

What the day actually feels like

I come in with a notepad and nothing else. No laptop, no tablet, no clipboard, no rubric, no score.

I want to say plainly why, because "we are coming to observe your staff" can land badly and it should not. This is not evaluation, and it sits completely separate from your appraisal process. Nothing I write ends up in anyone's file. I am there to watch people work and catch them doing something well, which is a different job entirely from rating them. Where the schedule allows, I sit down with people one to one afterward.
