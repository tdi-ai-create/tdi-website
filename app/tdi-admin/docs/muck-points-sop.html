Muck Points

The effort score on the sales board. What it measures, how it is calculated, and what it deliberately does not do.

Live on the sales board since 21 September 2026. Designed 15 to 17 September.

A muck point is a unit of predicted work. It answers one question: given two leads, which one should we pick up first. It is a prediction made before anyone has done the work, so it is a guide to sequencing and never a promise about a particular school.

Where to see it

Sales board, /tdi-admin/sales. Every lead carries a muck score. The board and the Outreach Queue both sort by value per muck point, with the deal value and the score shown side by side so you can always see what produced the order.

Open any lead and the detail panel shows one summary line. "What makes this score" expands it into the four dimensions.

Where the model actually lives

lib/sales/muck.ts, served by /api/sales/muck. That file is the source, this page is the explanation. If the two ever disagree, the file is right and this page is stale, so fix the page.

The four dimensions

One hundred points, split four ways. A heavier score means more predicted work, so a low score is a lead that is cheap to serve.

DimensionWeightWhat it reads
Delivery load 40 The offering on the record, and nothing else. Fixed per offering, not estimated per school.
Grant work 25 Whether this school needs funding help. Counted only once a person has confirmed it. Never predicted.
Relationship drag 20 How much back and forth this lead has taken, measured against the median for its own stage rather than against the whole board.
Travel 15 Taken from the state on the record, measured from the western Chicago suburbs. Full weight on a Blueprint or an unknown offering, half otherwise.

This is the largest single input, which means the offering field decides most of the score. A lead with no offering recorded cannot be ranked at all, and the board says so rather than guessing.

How a lead gets called heavy

By rank, never by a fixed cutoff. The board sorts every valued lead by score and then bands them.

Heaviest fifth = heavy→ Next 30 percent = moderate→ The rest = light

This is the whole defence against what killed the old score. The retired T1 fit score used fixed thresholds, and the result was that 41 percent of the board sat in the top tier while exactly one lead was T3. A label that applies to nearly half the board tells you nothing. Banding by rank means heavy always means heavy relative to everything else we are carrying right now.

Two findings that changed the model

A deal value before signature is a prediction

Rae, 17 September: the deal number is in the contract when it is signed, and until then it is all a prediction.

Measured across 58 unsigned leads carrying both a value and an offering: 42 were more than 1.5 times list price, and not one of the 35 Pulse values was plausible, the highest being 18,000 against a 2,500 list price. Most predate the offering restructure.

So: a recorded figure within 1.5 times list is kept, because somebody chose it deliberately. Anything beyond that falls back to list. The board headline moved from 1.59M to 1.24M on that change alone.

District size does not predict effort

Measured across the nine partnerships that carry any notes. Correlation of staff count to note volume was 0.038. Correlation of deliverables to note volume was 0.745.

So: size was cut from the model entirely. What we agreed to deliver predicts the work. How big the district is does not.

How it updates

It is not a score somebody sets. Nothing is stored. The whole thing is recalculated from the record every time the board loads, so there is no refresh to run and no way for it to go stale.

It reads five fields and nothing else:

InputRead fromChanges when
Delivery load, 40The offering on the leadSomebody sets or changes the offering
Grant, 25The grant support flagSomebody confirms the school needs funding help
Relationship drag, 20The number of notesAny note is added
Travel, 15The state on the leadThe state is filled in or corrected
Value per pointThe deal value and the stageThe value is edited, or the deal signs

Notes count, but nobody reads them. Drag is driven by how many notes a lead has, never by what they say. Logging a call adds one whether the call went brilliantly or nobody picked up. Write notes for the humans who need them, not to move the score.

The two fields that actually need you

Offering and grant support, together worth 65 of the 100 points. Both are set by a person and neither is ever guessed.

Grant in particular is only counted once somebody has confirmed the school needs funding help. Until then it reads Not confirmed and scores zero. Tick it and the lead gains 25 points immediately, which will usually move it down the chase order. That is deliberate: a predicted grant need was the input most likely to be wrong, so the model waits to be told.

One thing that surprises people

A lead's drag can change without anything happening to that lead.

Drag compares a lead's note count to the median for its own stage, and that median is recalculated across the whole board on every load. A lead showing 3 notes, stage median 11 scores zero drag today. If the other leads in its stage go quiet and the median falls, the same three notes can start scoring.

This is intended. Note counts climb with stage, measured at 9.4 in Quote Sent against 3.6 in Targeting on 15 September, so comparing a lead to the whole board would mostly measure how far along it is rather than how demanding it is. The cost of getting that right is that drag is relative, and relative things move.

What it is not

It is not a capacity plan. It does not know who is free this week or how many leads one person can carry.

It is not pricing. A heavy lead is not a lead we should charge more for. It is a lead that will cost us more to serve at whatever price we agreed.

It is not a quality or fit judgement. A heavy school can be a wonderful partner. The score has no opinion about whether we want them.

It is not split by owner. The board reads "TDI admin load, factored by stage". Human work here is one role, not two people's queues.

Changing the model

lib/sales/muck.ts is frozen until 28 September 2026. No new dimensions and no weight changes before then. Bug fixes outside that file are fine.

The freeze exists so the first week of real use is measured against one stable model. A score that moves while people are learning to read it teaches nobody anything.

Where the queue order felt wrong to you is the actual input to the 28 September review. Write those down as they happen and bring them. That is more useful than a weight opinion in the abstract.
