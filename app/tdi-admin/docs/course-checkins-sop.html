Course Check-Ins SOP

What a check-in is, how many a course gets, and how to create them

Last updated: August 13, 2026

What a check-in is

A check-in is a pause inside a course. It is one thing made of two parts, not a single question:

  1. A comprehension question that confirms the idea from that lesson landed. Multiple choice, four options, one correct, with an explanation that teaches why.
  2. A reflection that asks the educator to connect the idea to their own classroom, or, on the last check-in of a course, an implementation plan: what they will try, with which students or colleagues, and when.

The educator answers both parts before the course lets them continue. A check-in that only asks a question is not finished.

Why both parts

A comprehension question alone proves someone watched. It does not change anything on Monday. The second part is where the course turns into practice, and it is the reason educators tell us the Hub is different from PD that ends with a quiz.

How many, and where they go

RuleDetail
CountThree per course. Fewer only when the course has fewer than three teaching lessons.
PlacementRoughly a third of the way in, two thirds in, and on the final content lesson.
Never onWelcome, Your Guide, Meet the Author, or a downloads packet. Those introduce the course, they do not teach it.
Last oneAlways the implementation plan, and always on the last real teaching lesson so the plan comes after the whole course.

Every check-in needs a transcript

Check-ins are written from the transcript of the lesson they sit on, so the question tests that lesson rather than a vague summary of the course. A lesson with no transcript cannot carry a check-in, and a course with no transcripts cannot be published.

See the Course Upload SOP for how to generate transcripts.

How to create them

  1. Confirm the course has transcripts. Learning Hub > Production > Courses, open the course, check the lessons.
  2. Click Generate check-ins on the course.
  3. Wait. It reads each lesson's transcript and writes a question plus a reflection or plan for each of the three positions.
  4. Read what it produced. You are the editor, not a rubber stamp.

What to check before moving on

  • The question is answerable only by someone who watched that lesson. If common sense gets you there, it is a weak question.
  • The three wrong options are plausible. Obviously wrong options teach nothing.
  • The reflection asks about something specific from the lesson, not "how do you feel about communication."
  • The final plan names a real strategy from the course, so it cannot be answered with "I will try harder."
  • No dashes, no emojis, and it reads like a colleague talking to a colleague.

Regenerating is safe

Running Generate check-ins again replaces the current set. The old questions are retired rather than deleted, so any answers educators already gave stay attached to the question they answered. Nothing is lost and nothing is orphaned.

Where the answers go

Everything an educator writes is stored against their account in hub_quiz_responses. Comprehension answers, reflections, and plans.

Reflections are private, and we say so out loud

The player tells educators "This is just for you. We will never share your reflections." Two things follow, and both are commitments, not preferences:

  • Individual reflections and plans are never shown to school administrators, principals, or partners. The Champion page promises this in writing.
  • School-level reporting uses counts and themes with no names attached. How many educators completed check-ins, how many wrote plans. Never the words themselves.

Known gap, as of August 2026

Reflections and plans are stored but are not yet shown back to the educator who wrote them. Giving educators their own words back, on the course completion page or with their certificate, is planned and not built. Until it is, treat a reflection as written into a drawer that only opens later.

Troubleshooting

What you seeWhat it means
"No lesson with a usable transcript"The course has no transcripts, or they are too short. Generate transcripts first.
A course shows fewer than three check-insIt has fewer than three teaching lessons with transcripts. Usually correct. Confirm the lessons that are missing transcripts are meant to be missing them.
The last check-in is not on the last lessonThe final lesson has no usable transcript, so the plan moved to the last lesson that does. Add a transcript to move it back.
