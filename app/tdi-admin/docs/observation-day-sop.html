Observation Day SOP

How TDI runs an on-campus observation day, from the hold on the calendar to the dashboard update

Last updated 16 September 2026

An observation day is a celebration-first classroom visit. We name what each educator does well, offer one practical next step, and send it to them the same day. It is never an evaluation, and nothing we write is scored. This SOP is the internal version of the client-facing Observation Day Guide.

The one rule

Every educator we visit gets a personal Love Note before we leave the building. Not most. Every one. If we cannot get to a room, we do not pretend we did, and we tell the leader which rooms we missed.

What it is, and what it is not

What it is

  • Purpose. Naming strengths and building on them
  • Tone. Warm, specific, plain-spoken
  • Output. A personal Love Note to each educator, same day
  • Growth. One small, optional next step
  • Leader sees. Themes, standout moments, what is next

What it is not

  • Purpose. Rating, ranking, or compliance checks
  • Tone. Clinical, generic, or gotcha
  • Output. A score sheet or report card
  • Growth. A list of everything that went wrong
  • Leader sees. A ranking of individual staff

Step 0. Book and hold the date

As soon as a date is agreed on a call, before anything else moves:

  • Create the internal hold, 6:00am to 8:00pm in the school's timezone, titled [School Name] Observation Day [N]. Invite rae@ and hello@. This covers travel either side, not just the school day.
  • Create the client-facing all day event, titled [School Name] Teachers Deserve it Visit [N]. Invite the leader and hello@. Mark it free so it does not block their day. Put Times: TBD at the top of the notes with the general run of the day.
  • Add the date to timeline_events on the partnership so it shows on their dashboard. The leader should never have to email us to find out when we are coming.
  • If the date is tentative, say so in the event and on the timeline. Check it against the district calendar, especially spring break, before calling it firm.

Step 1. Plan it with the leader

  • Ask for the bell schedule, specials and lunch, and anything happening that day that would make a room a bad visit.
  • Ask what they want us watching for. Tie it to their partnership goals so the visit produces baseline data, not just good feeling.
  • Send back a route with a time for every room. Confirm who is a paraprofessional. Without that, any para goal has no denominator.
  • Confirm every staff member has Hub access before the visit. Fix seats and roster links first, not after.

Step 2. Visit classrooms

  • Short, quiet pop-ins of 10 to 20 minutes.
  • Notepad only. Eyes up, fully present, no laptops in classrooms.
  • Write exact words and moves. Quotes are what make a Love Note land. Never write a score.
  • Teaching keeps going. We do not interrupt, and we do not ask the adult to perform for us.

Step 3. Close the loop

  • Every educator gets their Love Note that day, before we leave.
  • Stay on campus for follow-up conversations. Tell people where we are and until when.
  • Photograph the notepad pages and upload to the Leadership Dashboard so the AI can draft and the data lands in the partnership record.
  • Tag every educator observed with strategy tags and growth indicators. This is what turns a nice day into measurable goal progress.

What we look for

Eight areas guide every visit, for every educator in the building.

AreaWhat good looks likeIf it is not there yet
Relationships and belongingStudents joke, share freely, and choose to sit near the adult. The adult knows students as people.Greet by name at the door. One personal check-in each period.
Clear directions and transitionsDirections in small steps with predictable cues, so students know what is next.Post the next step where everyone can see it.
Voice, tone and calm presenceA low steady voice, patience under pressure, resets without frustration.Permission to pause and reset, plus a few ready-made redirection phrases.
Questioning and student thinkingThe adult asks instead of tells, uses question stems, and waits for answers.Swap one tell for an ask, and count to five before helping.
Checking for understandingQuick checks that reach most students, and feedback that is specific.Aim for a response from every student, not just raised hands.
Engagement and joySongs, games, movement and humor tied to the learning.One game-based review each week.
Visible learning and environmentAgenda posted, modeling on screen, organized systems, readable slides.Bigger on-screen text and a simple copy this, then do this.
Individual support and teamworkSmart seating, gradual release, home-language support, smooth handoffs between teachers, paras and specialists.One whole-group question when it gets busy: what should we be doing right now?

Quality bar. A strong Love Note names three to seven of these moments, each with a quote or a concrete detail. "Great job with the kids" does not count and should not go out.

The Love Note

Same six parts, same order, every time. 250 to 400 words. Short enough to finish.

  1. A personal opening. Their name and the moment we walked into.
  2. What I Noticed. Three to seven specific strengths, with quotes.
  3. One Thing to Try. A single small idea, offered as an option.
  4. Tools for You. One or two free resources that fit what we saw.
  5. Your 3-Minute Next Step. One question they can answer by hitting reply.
  6. Let's Keep Talking. Where we will be, and until when.

Subject line is identical for everyone. So no note stands out in an inbox and nobody reads a ranking into it.

Copy the leaders named in the plan. Never other staff. An educator's note is theirs.

Turning the visit into goal data

Observation days are the measurement instrument for instructional goals. Platform metrics like login rate are hygiene, not goals. Tag every educator observed in the observation tool so the visit produces comparable data.

Strategy tags

Proximity, Small Group Facilitation, De-escalation, Checking for Understanding, Routine Building, Communication with Teacher, Student Engagement, Differentiation, Positive Reinforcement, Hub Content in Action, Independent Initiative, Questioning Techniques.

Growth indicators

Saw Confidence, Saw Independence, Saw Collaboration, Saw Hub Content Applied, Saw Student Responsiveness, Saw Reflection and Self-Awareness.

Baseline then growth

The first visit of the year sets the baseline. The second measures movement against it. Do not promise a partner a target number before the first visit. Agree targets with them once both sides know the starting point. Saying "I would rather set these with you once we know where you are actually starting from" is a stronger conversation than a number invented in September.

Before every visit, check these

  • Roster is real. Audit from auth.users by email domain, never from hub_profiles.email, which is null on self-registered accounts and will silently hide staff.
  • Everyone has a seat. Contracted seats minus provisioned seats is not the number of people missing access. Check who is actually logging in.
  • Roles are known. Hub roles are self-selected and frequently wrong. Get the para list from the leader in writing.
  • Goals are on the dashboard with a note explaining how each one is measured.
  • Nothing is owed. Check billing before the visit so a money question on the day gets a straight answer, not a guess.
